# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

TraceSleuth is a technical open-source project. We are committed to a professional, respectful, and harassment-free environment for contributors and users regardless of background, experience level, identity, disability, appearance, nationality, age, religion, or other personal characteristics.

Technical rigor and direct criticism of code, protocol interpretation, detector methodology, security posture, tests, and documentation are welcome. Personal attacks are not.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- be respectful and professional;
- focus criticism on technical work rather than individuals;
- provide evidence for protocol and detector claims;
- acknowledge uncertainty and limitations;
- correct mistakes openly;
- respect upstream attribution and contributor work;
- avoid publishing private packet captures, credentials, personal data, or security exploit details irresponsibly;
- accept that maintainers may reject technically unsafe or insufficiently tested changes;
- help make reviews constructive and reproducible.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or discrimination;
- personal insults or sustained hostility;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- deliberate disclosure of private information;
- publishing confidential packet captures or credentials without authorization;
- knowingly fabricating test results, packet evidence, standards citations, vendor behavior, or detector capability;
- attempting to pressure maintainers to hide known security or correctness problems;
- other conduct reasonably considered inappropriate in a professional open-source community.

## Technical disagreement

TraceSleuth deals with uncertain network evidence. Good-faith disagreement is expected.

Resolve technical disputes through:

- packet references;
- minimal fixtures;
- standards text;
- vendor documentation;
- Wireshark behavior;
- reproducible tests;
- measured benchmarks;
- explicit alternative explanations.

The goal is not to win an argument. The goal is to improve the correctness and credibility of the product.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers may edit, hide, reject, or remove comments, issues, pull requests, code, or contributions that violate this policy. Repeated or severe violations may result in temporary or permanent participation restrictions.

Enforcement decisions should be proportional, documented internally where appropriate, and focused on protecting contributors and project integrity.

## Reporting conduct issues

Use a private project communication mechanism when available. Do not publicly disclose sensitive personal information as part of a conduct report.

Security vulnerabilities should be reported through `SECURITY.md`, not through the conduct process.

## Scope

This policy applies in project-managed spaces and when representing the TraceSleuth project publicly.

## Attribution

This policy is project-authored and intentionally concise. Future adoption of a standardized code-of-conduct template may be recorded explicitly with its version and attribution.
